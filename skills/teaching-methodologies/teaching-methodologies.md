Curriculum: AI-Assisted Interactive Pedagogy

Module 1: The Socratic Tutor (Dialectic Method)

- Concept: Utilizing the AI as a relentless questioner rather than an answer-machine.
- Workflow: Instead of asking for summaries, you present a premise and command the AI to find flaws, contradictions, or missing variables in your logic.
- Objective: To sharpen analytical rigor and identify blind spots in your worldview.

Module 2: The Feynman Technique (Iterative Simplification)

- Concept: Teaching a complex topic to the AI to uncover gaps in your own understanding.
- Workflow: Draft an explanation of a concept. Ask the AI to act as a "Critical Peer Reviewer" to grade your accuracy, clarity, and depth. Use the AI’s feedback to refine your next iteration.
- Objective: To achieve high-fidelity retention and mastery by transforming passive input into active output.

Module 3: Case-Based Simulation (Experiential Learning)

- Concept: Immersing yourself in specific scenarios to apply abstract theory.
- Workflow: Provide the AI with a structured set of variables (e.g., a historical crisis, a technical coding error, or a philosophical dilemma). The AI acts as the environment responding to your decisions.
- Objective: To transition from rote knowledge to practical intuition and decision-making capacity.

Module 4: Guided Discovery (Heuristic Frameworks)

- Concept: Avoiding direct answers in favor of mental models.
- Workflow: Ask the AI to withhold the "answer" to your problem and instead provide three distinct mental models or historical precedents that would help you solve it.
- Objective: To build your own cognitive toolkit rather than relying on the AI’s specific outputs.

Module 5: Hybrid Synthesis (The Workflow Integration)

- Concept: Combining methods for a comprehensive study cycle.
- Workflow: Use Case-Based Simulation to generate data, apply the Feynman Technique to articulate the mechanics behind that data, and conclude with the Socratic Method to test the limits of your conclusions.
